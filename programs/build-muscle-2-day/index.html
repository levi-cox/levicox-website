Your training week.

  1. Day 1Full Body A
  2. Day 2Full Body B

Both days train your whole body, so every muscle gets worked twice a week even though you only lift twice. Full Body A and Full Body B are two different sessions, which spreads the work across more movements. Put a couple of days between them, Monday and Thursday for example, so you come into each one recovered.

Size comes from hard sets, done often, with weights that keep going up. These programs spread that work across your week: full body sessions at 2 and 3 days, upper and lower at 4 days and a body-part split at 5.

Who it's for.

Beginners, or anyone coming back after a break, who can reliably make two gym sessions a week. Sessions run longer, about 75 minutes, because each one covers the whole body. If you can find a third day, the 3-day version trains each muscle more often.

Questions.

How long is each session?

About 75 min a session. Programs with fewer days have slightly longer sessions because each day covers more.

Is it suitable for beginners?

Yes. The 2 and 3-day programs are written for beginners.

How long does the program run?

8 weeks.

What should I do when I finish?

Move up to Build Muscle: 3-Day Full Body when your schedule allows, or run this one again starting from heavier weights.

I post free exercise demos on my YouTube channel, so you can check your form on any movement in the program.
