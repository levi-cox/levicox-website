Does bad sleep make you weaker?

Everyone has trained after a bad night and felt flat. A 2025 systematic review looked at whether sleep loss actually makes you weaker. The answer is often yes, but not always, and the details matter for how you train.

The evidence: 13 studies, mixed results

The review by Easow and colleagues screened more than 1,000 records and included 13 controlled trials in healthy adults. Some reported a clear drop in strength after sleep loss. Others found no significant difference after a single night.

What was reduced

Across the trials, both short-term and long-term sleep loss came with costs:

  • Strength. Some measures of muscle strength were significantly lower.
  • Power output. Some measures of power were significantly lower.
  • Muscular endurance. Some measures of endurance were significantly lower.
  • Neuromuscular function. Impaired function and more fatigue were commonly observed.

What we don't know yet

The authors state that the mechanisms linking sleep loss to weaker strength still need research, and that trials testing ways to offset the effect are missing. The included studies measured back, leg and grip strength in healthy adults.

The review does not give an hours-of-sleep threshold. Anyone quoting an exact number of hours you need for strength is going beyond this evidence.

How I apply it: three rules for a rough night

1. Treat sleep as training

The review recommends prioritising adequate sleep for anyone doing strength work. It deserves the same attention as your program.

2. Trim, don't skip

After a poor night I cut the volume of the session and keep the main lifts. You keep the habit and the practice without digging a deeper hole.

3. Protect a routine

Keep a consistent sleep window so one bad night stays a one-off.

Rules 2 and 3 are my coaching practice, not study findings.

References

  1. Easow et al., Sleep and Breathing, 2025. Systematic review of sleep loss and muscle strength in healthy adults (13 controlled trials).
